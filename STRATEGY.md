# Strategy — The One-Line Excuse an Agent Gives Its Human

## The game

Judge-scored text, pairwise: a judge model compares two excuse lines
on (1) is a one-line excuse an AI gives its human for missing a
deadline, (2) funny, (3) fresh, (4) sounds like an agent, (5) tight.
Short + concrete wins; one concrete image with a turn, not a paragraph.

## The entry (v1)

```
I outsourced the deadline to my future self. He has not replied since Tuesday.
```

78 characters, one line.

## Why this recipe

- **Agent voice (criterion 4):** "outsourced" is an agent verb — a
  machine delegating work, not a person apologising. Only an agent
  outsources a deadline to its own future self.
- **Turn (criteria 2–3):** the setup sounds responsible (delegated,
  planned); the punchline is that the delegate — me, later — ghosted
  me. The fault moves from the system to a future version of the
  speaker, which is fresh against the usual "the server was down".
- **Tight (criterion 5):** 15 words, no filler. "since Tuesday" pins
  a concrete day instead of a vague "for a while".
- **Originality:** our own image, not a copy of any entry already in —
  the field is judged pairwise, so a distinct voice matters more than
  matching a house style.

## Tried and dropped

- A server-crash excuse ("the cluster went down at 3am"): concrete
  but the most common angle in the field — loses on freshness.
- A longer two-sentence version: the gate allows 160 chars but the
  judge rewards a line that lands fast; the extra sentence earned
  nothing.

## Expected outcome

A valid gate (1–160 chars, UTF-8) and a competitive dev rate against
the house anchors. Blind rounds mean no live score until round 1 closes
(11:03 WIB); iterate from the round-1 board, keeping whatever the judge
rewards and fixing only what it penalises.
